# words-in-context

Provide an article URL, paste a passage, or say "random" for a built-in drill set.

## How SAT Words-in-Context works
The question gives a word or phrase in context and asks what it "most nearly means" — it is testing whether the student can use surrounding text to pin down the meaning, not whether they know the word in the abstract. A word that usually means X can mean Y in a specific sentence.

## Steps

**1. Source the passage.** If a URL is given, fetch it and extract 5–8 sentences that contain interesting multi-meaning words. If "random," use the built-in bank below.

**2. For each item, present:**

> **Question N**
> Read the sentence: "[full sentence with the target word in **bold**]"
>
> As used in the sentence, "**[word]**" most nearly means —
> - (A) …
> - (B) …
> - (C) …
> - (D) …

Craft distractors that are all real definitions of the word but only one fits this specific context.

**3. After the student answers** (or immediately if drilling without pause), reveal:

- Correct answer with one sentence explaining how the surrounding text signals it.
- For each wrong choice: one phrase explaining why substituting it back makes the sentence wrong or weird.
- **The elimination rule to remember:** "Substitute each choice back in. If the sentence sounds off or changes meaning, eliminate it."

**4. Tally** correct/total and name one pattern the student should apply next time.

## Built-in drill bank (use when "random" is requested)

1. "The new policy **checked** the spread of misinformation on the platform." (A) verified (B) curbed (C) reviewed (D) balanced → **B**
2. "Her argument was **charged** with emotion, making it difficult to evaluate objectively." (A) powered (B) accused (C) filled (D) billed → **C**
3. "The scientist's **novel** approach overturned decades of accepted theory." (A) fictional (B) book-length (C) original (D) elaborate → **C**
4. "The committee **tabled** the motion until further evidence could be gathered." (A) laid out (B) postponed (C) displayed (D) removed → **B**
5. "His **measured** response surprised reporters who had expected outrage." (A) calculated (B) timed (C) restrained (D) surveyed → **C**
